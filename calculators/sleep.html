💡 Sleep Science
Each sleep cycle lasts approximately 90 minutes and includes light sleep, deep sleep, and REM sleep. Waking up at the end of a cycle (light sleep phase) feels natural and refreshed. Waking mid-cycle causes sleep inertia — the groggy, disoriented feeling even after enough total sleep.

How the Sleep Calculator Works

Sleep happens in 90-minute cycles. Waking up at the end of a complete cycle, during the lightest sleep phase, minimizes sleep inertia. This calculator suggests bedtimes (or wake times) aligned to complete 4, 5, or 6 full cycles — corresponding to 6, 7.5, or 9 hours of sleep.

How many hours of sleep do adults need? ▾
The National Sleep Foundation recommends 7–9 hours for adults (18–64), 7–8 hours for older adults (65+), and 8–10 hours for teenagers. Less than 6 hours consistently is associated with increased risk of obesity, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and cognitive impairment. Quality matters as much as quantity — uninterrupted sleep through complete cycles is more restorative than fragmented longer sleep.
Why do I feel worse after 8 hours than 7.5? ▾
You likely woke up mid-cycle. 8 hours doesn't divide evenly into 90-minute cycles (8h = 5.33 cycles). 7.5 hours = exactly 5 cycles, so you wake up at the natural end of a cycle in light sleep. This is why 7.5 hours often feels more refreshing than 8 or 8.5 hours.
