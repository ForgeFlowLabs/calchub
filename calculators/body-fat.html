What Is Body Fat Percentage?

Body fat percentage is the proportion of your total body weight that is fat tissue. The remainder — muscle, bone, organs, water, and connective tissue — is called lean mass. Unlike BMI, body fat percentage directly measures body composition, making it a more useful health indicator for athletes and people with high muscle mass.

The US Navy Method Formula

This calculator uses the US Navy circumference method, developed for military fitness assessment:

Men: 86.010×log₁₀(waist−neck) − 70.041×log₁₀(height) + 36.76
Women: 163.205×log₁₀(waist+hip−neck) − 97.684×log₁₀(height) − 78.387

All measurements in inches (or converted from cm). The method is validated against hydrostatic weighing and DEXA scan, with a typical error of ±3–4%.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is the Navy body fat method? ▾
The US Navy method is typically accurate to within 3–4 percentage points of a DEXA scan for most people. It's more accurate than BMI (which doesn't measure fat at all) and more accessible than skinfold calipers (which require trained technique) or hydrostatic weighing (which requires a special tank). The main source of error is measurement technique — ensure you measure correctly for best accuracy.
What is a healthy body fat percentage? ▾
For men, the fitness range is 8–19% body fat. For women, 21–33% is considered healthy. Athletes typically have lower percentages: 6–13% for male athletes and 14–20% for female athletes. Going below essential fat levels (2–5% for men, 10–13% for women) is dangerous and associated with hormonal disruption, immune dysfunction, and organ damage.
Why do women have higher body fat than men at the same fitness level? ▾
Women naturally carry more body fat due to higher estrogen levels and physiological requirements — particularly for reproductive health. The essential fat required for basic physiological function is 10–13% in women vs. 2–5% in men. This is why female body fat categories are set 8–10 percentage points higher than male categories across all fitness levels.
What is the most accurate way to measure body fat? ▾
DEXA (Dual-Energy X-ray Absorptiometry) scan is considered the gold standard, with accuracy to within 1–2%. It also measures bone density and shows regional fat distribution. Hydrostatic weighing (underwater weighing) is similarly accurate. Skinfold calipers done by an expert are within 3–5%. Smart scales using bioelectrical impedance (BIA) are the least accurate, often off by 5–8%. The Navy method falls between calipers and DEXA in accuracy and accessibility.
Can I lower my body fat percentage without losing weight? ▾
Yes — this is called body recomposition. By maintaining a slight caloric deficit (or at maintenance) while doing resistance training, you can lose fat while building or maintaining muscle. The scale may stay the same or even increase, while body fat percentage drops. Recomposition is slower than dedicated cutting but preserves more muscle. It works best for beginners, people returning after a break, and those who are slightly overweight.
