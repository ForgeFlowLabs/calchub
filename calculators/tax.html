2025 Federal Income Tax Brackets

The US uses a progressive tax system — higher income is taxed at higher rates, but only the income within each bracket is taxed at that rate. Being in the 22% bracket doesn't mean you pay 22% on all your income — it means you pay 22% only on income within that bracket's range.

2025 Standard Deductions

The standard deduction for 2025 increased due to inflation adjustments: $15,000 for single filers, $30,000 for married filing jointly, and $22,500 for head of household. Most taxpayers benefit from taking the standard deduction rather than itemizing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between effective and marginal tax rate? ▾
Your marginal rate is the tax rate applied to your last dollar of income — your "tax bracket." Your effective rate is the average rate you actually pay across all income. Because the US has a progressive tax system, your effective rate is always lower than your marginal rate. For example, a single filer earning $80,000 might be in the 22% marginal bracket but have an effective federal rate of only about 14–15%, because their income below $48,475 is taxed at 10% and 12%.
Should I itemize or take the standard deduction? ▾
Take whichever gives you a larger deduction. The 2025 standard deduction ($15,000 single / $30,000 married) is high enough that about 90% of taxpayers benefit more from the standard deduction than itemizing. Itemizing makes sense if your deductible expenses exceed the standard amount — typically for high mortgage interest, large charitable donations, or significant medical expenses exceeding 7.5% of AGI.
What are FICA taxes? ▾
FICA (Federal Insurance Contributions Act) taxes fund Social Security and Medicare. Employees pay 6.2% for Social Security (on wages up to $176,100 in 2025) and 1.45% for Medicare on all wages — totaling 7.65%. Employers match this amount. Self-employed individuals pay both halves (15.3%) but can deduct the employer portion. There's also an Additional Medicare Tax of 0.9% on wages over $200,000 ($250,000 for married filing jointly).
How does a 401(k) reduce my taxes? ▾
Traditional 401(k) contributions are made pre-tax — they reduce your federal taxable income dollar for dollar. If you're in the 22% bracket and contribute $10,000, you save $2,200 in federal taxes that year. The 2025 401(k) contribution limit is $23,500 ($31,000 if 50+). This is one of the most powerful tax reduction strategies available to employees. Roth 401(k) contributions don't reduce taxes now but grow tax-free.
What tax credits can reduce what I owe? ▾
Tax credits directly reduce your tax bill — more valuable than deductions. Key 2025 credits include: Child Tax Credit ($2,000 per qualifying child under 17), Earned Income Tax Credit (up to $7,830 for low-to-moderate income earners), Child and Dependent Care Credit (up to $1,050 for one child), American Opportunity Credit ($2,500 for college education), and the Saver's Credit (up to $1,000 for retirement contributions with income under certain limits). This calculator does not include credits — your actual tax may be significantly lower.
