How Much Do You Need to Retire?

The most widely used guideline is the 25x rule: save 25 times your expected annual retirement spending. This is derived from the 4% rule — if you withdraw 4% of your portfolio annually, 25x your spending is exactly what you need. If you want $60,000/year in retirement, you need $1.5 million saved ($60,000 × 25).

The 4% Rule Explained

The 4% rule came from the Trinity Study (1998), which analyzed historical US market data and found that a 4% annual withdrawal rate had a 95%+ success rate of lasting 30 years across all historical periods. It's a starting point, not a guarantee — lower rates (3–3.5%) are safer for longer retirements or early retirees.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I save each month for retirement? ▾
The standard guideline is to save 15% of your gross income, including any employer 401(k) match. If you started late, aim for 20–25%. The earlier you start, the less you need to save monthly — because of compounding, starting at 25 vs. 35 with the same goal can require roughly half the monthly savings. Use this calculator to find your specific target.
What is the difference between a 401(k) and an IRA? ▾
A 401(k) is employer-sponsored — contributions come from your paycheck pre-tax, reducing your taxable income now. Many employers offer matching contributions (free money). The 2025 contribution limit is $23,500 ($31,000 if 50+). An IRA (Individual Retirement Account) is opened independently. Traditional IRA contributions may be tax-deductible. Roth IRA contributions are after-tax but withdrawals in retirement are tax-free. The 2025 IRA limit is $7,000 ($8,000 if 50+).
Should I use a Traditional or Roth account? ▾
If you expect to be in a higher tax bracket in retirement than you are now, Roth is better — you pay taxes now at a lower rate and all growth is tax-free. If you're in your peak earning years and expect lower income in retirement, Traditional is often better — you defer taxes to when your rate is lower. Many financial planners recommend having both to provide tax flexibility in retirement.
What is a safe withdrawal rate for early retirement? ▾
For a 30-year retirement, 4% is historically well-supported. For 40+ year retirements (early retirement / FIRE), most research suggests 3–3.5% is safer. The Kitces research shows that flexible spending rules — reducing withdrawals in down markets — significantly improve longevity. Many early retirees target a 3.5% rate and plan to adjust spending based on portfolio performance.
How does Social Security factor into retirement planning? ▾
Social Security reduces how much you need to withdraw from your portfolio each month. Enter your expected monthly benefit in this calculator to see the gap your savings needs to cover. You can check your estimated benefit at ssa.gov. Full retirement age for Social Security is 67 for those born after 1960. Delaying to age 70 increases your benefit by 8% per year — a guaranteed return hard to beat anywhere.
What is the FIRE movement? ▾
FIRE stands for Financial Independence, Retire Early. The core idea is to save aggressively (50–70% of income) to reach financial independence faster — typically by accumulating 25x annual expenses. Different variants include Lean FIRE (minimal spending), Fat FIRE (comfortable spending), and Barista FIRE (semi-retirement with part-time income). The math is the same as traditional retirement planning, just compressed.
