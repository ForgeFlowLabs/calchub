How Is a Due Date Calculated?

The most widely used method is Naegele's Rule: add 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP). This standard was established by German obstetrician Franz Karl Naegele in the 19th century and remains the basis for due date calculation worldwide. It assumes a 28-day cycle with ovulation occurring on day 14.

The Three Trimesters

Pregnancy is divided into three trimesters: the first trimester (weeks 1–12) covers early development, morning sickness, and the highest risk of miscarriage; the second trimester (weeks 13–26) is typically the most comfortable period with reduced nausea and visible growth; the third trimester (weeks 27–40) involves rapid fetal growth, preparation for birth, and increasing discomfort as the due date approaches.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is a due date calculator? ▾
Due date calculators provide a statistical estimate, not a precise prediction. Only about 5% of babies are born on their exact due date. Roughly 80% of births occur within 2 weeks before or after the estimated date. A first-trimester ultrasound (before 14 weeks) is the most accurate method for confirming gestational age — it can typically date a pregnancy to within ±5–7 days.
What if my cycle isn't 28 days? ▾
This calculator adjusts for cycle length. If your cycle is longer than 28 days, ovulation (and likely conception) occurs later, shifting your due date forward. If your cycle is shorter, the due date shifts earlier. For example, with a 35-day cycle, ovulation typically occurs on day 21 rather than day 14, adding 7 days to the standard Naegele's Rule calculation.
What is a full-term pregnancy? ▾
Full-term pregnancy is now defined as 39–40 weeks of gestation. Early term is 37–38 weeks, late term is 41 weeks, and post-term is 42+ weeks. Babies born before 37 weeks are considered premature (preterm). The ACOG (American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists) recommends against elective delivery before 39 weeks when possible, as the final weeks are critical for lung and brain development.
How is IVF due date calculated differently? ▾
For IVF pregnancies, the due date is calculated from the embryo transfer date plus the age of the embryo. For a Day 5 blastocyst transfer, the LMP equivalent is 19 days before the transfer date (transfer date − 5 days for embryo age − 14 days for the LMP-to-ovulation interval). Add 280 days from that LMP equivalent to get the due date. This calculator handles all three common transfer types (Day 3, 5, and 6).
What are the most important prenatal appointments? ▾
Key appointments include: First prenatal visit (6–8 weeks) for confirmation and blood work; nuchal translucency ultrasound (11–14 weeks) for chromosomal screening; anatomy scan (18–20 weeks) to check fetal development; glucose tolerance test (24–28 weeks) for gestational diabetes screening; Group B strep test (35–37 weeks); and weekly visits from 36 weeks until delivery.
