What Is BMR?

Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest — just to keep organs functioning, maintain body temperature, and keep you alive. It accounts for 60–75% of total daily energy expenditure. BMR is not the same as TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure), which adds calories burned through activity.

Which BMR formula is most accurate? ▾
The Mifflin-St Jeor equation (1990) is considered the most accurate for most people and is recommended by the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics. The Harris-Benedict equation (revised 1984) is slightly less accurate for modern populations but still widely used. For people with known body fat percentage, the Katch-McArdle formula is most precise.
How can I increase my BMR? ▾
Muscle tissue burns more calories at rest than fat tissue. Building muscle through resistance training is the most effective way to increase BMR over time. Adequate sleep, avoiding crash diets (which reduce lean mass), and staying hydrated also support metabolic rate.
