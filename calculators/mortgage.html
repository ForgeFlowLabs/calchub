How to Use This Mortgage Calculator

Enter your home price, down payment, interest rate, and loan term to get your estimated monthly payment. Optionally add property tax, home insurance, PMI, and HOA fees to see your full monthly cost. The amortization schedule shows exactly how much goes to principal vs. interest each year — and your ending balance.

The Mortgage Payment Formula

Every mortgage payment is calculated using the standard amortization formula:

M = P × [r(1+r)ⁿ] / [(1+r)ⁿ − 1]

Where P = loan principal (home price minus down payment), r = monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12), and n = total number of monthly payments (loan term in years × 12). For a $320,000 loan at 6.75% over 30 years: r = 0.5625%, n = 360 payments, M ≈ $2,076/month.

What Is an Amortization Schedule?

An amortization schedule shows each payment broken down into principal and interest. In the early years of a mortgage, the majority of your payment goes toward interest. Over time, the balance shifts — more goes to principal. By the final years of a 30-year mortgage, nearly all of your payment is reducing the loan balance. This is why paying extra toward principal early in the loan has an outsized impact on total interest paid.

What Does PMI Mean?

Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) is required by most lenders when your down payment is less than 20% of the home's value. PMI typically costs 0.5%–1.5% of the loan amount per year. On a $300,000 loan, that's $1,500–$4,500/year or $125–$375/month. Once you reach 20% equity in your home, you can request PMI removal — which is why many buyers target a 20% down payment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good interest rate for a mortgage? ▾
In 2026, the average 30-year fixed mortgage rate is approximately 6.5–7%. A "good" rate depends on your credit score, down payment, loan type, and lender. Borrowers with credit scores above 760 and 20% down typically qualify for the best rates. Even a 0.5% difference in rate can save $30,000+ over 30 years on a $400,000 loan.
How much house can I afford? ▾
The standard rule is that your total housing costs (mortgage, tax, insurance) should not exceed 28% of your gross monthly income. Your total debt payments (housing + car + student loans) should stay below 36–43%. On a $100,000 annual income (~$8,333/month), your max housing payment would be ~$2,333/month under the 28% rule.
Is a 15-year or 30-year mortgage better? ▾
A 15-year mortgage has higher monthly payments but saves enormously on total interest — typically $100,000+ on a $400,000 loan. 15-year rates are also usually 0.5–0.75% lower than 30-year rates. A 30-year mortgage has lower monthly payments, giving more cash flow flexibility. The right choice depends on your income stability, other financial goals, and risk tolerance.
Should I make extra principal payments? ▾
Making extra principal payments reduces your loan balance faster and dramatically cuts total interest. Adding just $200/month to a $300,000 loan at 6.75% can shorten the term by 5–6 years and save over $80,000 in interest. Always confirm with your lender that extra payments are applied to principal with no prepayment penalty.
What's the difference between pre-qualification and pre-approval? ▾
Pre-qualification is an informal estimate of how much you might borrow, based on self-reported information. Pre-approval is a formal process where the lender verifies your income, credit, and assets — and issues a conditional loan commitment. Pre-approval is much stronger when making an offer on a home.
What closing costs should I expect? ▾
Closing costs typically run 2–5% of the loan amount. On a $320,000 loan, expect $6,400–$16,000 in closing costs. These include origination fees, appraisal, title insurance, escrow fees, and prepaid items like property tax and homeowner's insurance. Some lenders offer "no closing cost" loans that roll fees into the rate.
How does credit score affect my mortgage rate? ▾
Credit scores have a significant impact on mortgage rates. A score of 760+ typically gets the best rates. Dropping from 760 to 680 can add 0.5–1% to your rate — on a $300,000 loan, that's an extra $100–$200/month and $40,000–$80,000 over 30 years. Improving your credit score before applying can be one of the highest-ROI financial moves you can make.
What is an escrow account? ▾
An escrow account is held by your lender to collect and pay your property taxes and homeowner's insurance on your behalf. Each month, a portion of your mortgage payment goes into escrow. Your lender then pays the annual tax and insurance bills directly. Most lenders require escrow accounts, especially for loans with less than 20% down.
